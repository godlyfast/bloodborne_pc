# Night of 25 October 2026: layer overhead on the PC, Deck and laptop

Everything is local, **nothing was published** (git, releases and GitHub were not touched).

## Results in numbers

| Where | Before | After | What did it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steam Deck, standing in Fishing Hamlet (FSR 4.1.1) | 68.6 FPS, GPU 1345 MHz | ~71 FPS, 1400 MHz | no draw-preparation threads, fetch-shader decode cache |
| Deck, stage A per draw | 15.3 µs | 13.9 µs | fetch-shader decode cache |
| Laptop, frame presentation (spread) | 36–45 ms, 3 frames in a burst | 8–11 ms, frame by frame | FIFO instead of Mailbox on NVIDIA/Windows |
| Laptop, 1080p FSR 1 | 29–30 FPS | 31.6–31.9 FPS | no preparation threads |
| PC | 162–164 FPS (yesterday) | 167–172 FPS | overnight changes, PGO re-recorded |

## What was done

### Frame presentation on the laptop (no limiter)
- Cause of the stutter: **Mailbox on NVIDIA under Windows**. `vkAcquireNextImageKHR` blocked for ~100 ms when the GPU was busy, then three frames were shown in a row.
- Now on Windows with NVIDIA, Mailbox is automatically replaced with FIFO (the log has the line `Swapchain: Mailbox -> FIFO on NVIDIA`). FPS does not change. To keep Mailbox: `BB_NVIDIA_MAILBOX=1`.
- The remaining slow frames (~5–8%) are our threads being preempted by the game's threads on 4 cores.
  - Raising the priority of our threads (`BB_STAGE_PRIORITY=1`) removes them (+3% FPS, half as many long frames).
  - But with it, during loading the game more often handed over an incompletely written command buffer ("Bad PM4 packet": 3 crashes out of 10 versus 1 out of 15). So it is **off by default**.

### CPU overhead
- **Draw-preparation threads are disabled on the Deck (integrated GPU with a shared power budget) and on CPUs with fewer than 8 threads (the laptop).** With 8–11 threads and a discrete GPU, 1 thread remains; from 12 threads, a quarter of the threads (on the PC, without them FPS is −21%). On the Deck the single thread managed to prepare only ~13% of the draws, and stage A redid the rest anyway. And its time ate into the shared 15 W budget.
- **Fetch-shader decode cache**: the check on every draw decoded it anew (~5% of stage A on the Deck).
- **`StageSpecialization::Matches`**: shader variant lookup without building the specialization. Verified on 15.5 million lookups, 0 mismatches. The gain is small (13.2 → 11.5%).
- **CLEAR_STATE**: registers are restored only in the touched blocks, not the whole 208 KB file (~63 times per frame for each stage). Verified with `BB_REGS_CHECK=1` and a stage comparison.
- **GPU progress polling** is throttled via `rdtsc` rather than `clock_gettime`.
- **PGO** was re-recorded for the new code.

### Heap shrinking (spots, missing reflection)
- Cause found: when the game released a block, the textures in it were deleted only at the end of a batch of frames. In that time the game managed to hand out the same address again, and the GPU to render something new there. The deferred deletion erased the textures that were already new.
- Now the deletion happens before the first submission made after the release. On the Deck, with the old order 139 of 143 ranges were late (94 MB of textures rendered by the GPU), with the new one 0.
- **Shrinking is enabled by default again** (`BB_HEAP_SHRINK=0` turns it off).

### Reactive mask and motion vectors
- In the sewers and near water, two G-buffer passes run per frame: the scene at 640×400 and the reflection at 320×200 with its own camera.
- The last pass was taken, i.e. the reflection: both the camera for the motion vectors and the color for the mask.
- Now the largest pass of the frame is taken. **Not verified in the sewers.**

### Removed
- Texture preloading (it did not work; files are in `../bbport-archive/texture-preload-2026-10-25`).

## Checked and discarded
- Memoization of specializations by expanded data: 5% hits, buffer addresses are different for every object.
- A cache of recent reads in front of `PendingReads`: no gain on the PC.
- Disabling robustness (`BB_ROBUSTNESS=0`): no gain on the laptop.
- Mirror idle threshold (`BB_VRAM_PRESSURE_IDLE_SECONDS=30`) and flush interval (`BB_HONEST_FLUSH_US`): noise.
- Flushing work in the middle of a submission when the GPU is idle (`BB_IDLE_FLUSH_DRAWS=32`): on the laptop 30.7 versus 30.5 FPS (5 pairs, noise); on the PC −4% without a limit, neutral with a limit of once per 500 µs. Off by default.

## Where each device is bottlenecked
- **Deck**: the GPU and the 15 W limit (the GPU is 97% loaded). Every percent of CPU saved goes into GPU clock speed.
- **Laptop**: the GPU (91–95% even at 540p). Between submissions the GPU sits idle for ~3.3 ms per frame (~10%).
- **PC**: stage A (`GpuComm`, idle 1%).
- Stage B on the laptop and the Deck costs 10–15 µs per draw versus 2.7 on the PC: this is the **CPU cache size** (6 and 4 MB of L3 versus 96 MB on the 5700X3D), not Windows. Next comes the compactness of our data structures, a separate large piece of work.

## Diagnostics (all through environment variables)
`BB_TIMELINE` (new events: frame processing, present, acquire, who called the submission), `BB_SECTIONS=1 BB_SECTIONS_SLOW=1` (what is expensive in slow frames), `BB_REGS_CHECK=1`, `BB_SPEC_MATCH_CHECK=1`, `BB_DEAD_IMAGES_LATE=1` (the old deletion order, for comparison), `BB_SWAPCHAIN_IMAGES=N`.

## What you should check
- Deck: teleports and long play with heap shrinking: that there are no spots and the reflection is in place.
- Deck: sewers and water with FSR: anti-aliasing and shimmer.
- Laptop: frame pacing without the limiter.

- Stability: during loading, "Bad PM4 packet" occasionally appears (a bug of class #32, the game hands over a corrupted command buffer). With stage priority 1, 3 of 10 runs crashed; with 0 (the default), 1 of ~47.

## Laptop: "good" and "bad" mode
- Runs of the same build gave either 32–33 FPS (spread 5–8 ms) or 28–29 FPS (13–16 ms). In the bad ones the same CPU work takes one and a half times longer: stage A 14.4 µs per draw versus 8.5–10.5. This is a slowdown of the CPU itself (overheating after hours of runs, or background load), not the code.
- **Windows Defender** scans the game: the `D:\Bloodborne` folder is not in the exclusions, `MsMpEng` has accumulated ~2.4 hours of CPU time and takes ~21% of a core while the game loads. On 4 cores this is noticeable. **I recommend adding `D:\Bloodborne` to the Defender exclusions.** I did not change it myself: it is a security setting.
